## 2. Indicator Selection Rationale (8 indicators chosen)

For a **persistent downtrend with elevated volatility and a possible distribution-to-capitulation transition**, I selected indicators that cover four non-redundant dimensions:

1. **Trend (close_50_sma, close_200_sma, close_10_ema)** — Three timeframes answer the question "is this a tradeable trend or a bounce?" The 200 SMA defines the strategic regime; the 50 SMA is the first major overhead supply; the 10 EMA captures the near-term velocity of the current decline/bounce.
2. **Momentum (macd, macdh, rsi)** — MACD + histogram show whether downside momentum is accelerating or decelerating (critical after the 8/17 breakdown), while RSI flags oversold/overbought extremes and divergence. I intentionally **excluded the MACD signal line as a standalone pick** (redundant with macd/macdh) and **excluded stochastics/other oscillators** (would duplicate RSI's role).
3. **Volatility (atr)** — In a high-volatility, post-shock tape, ATR is essential for stop placement and position sizing (currently ~3.1% of price). I chose ATR over Bollinger bands in the indicator set to avoid crowding the trend dimension, though the verified snapshot's Bollinger values are still used as reference levels below.
4. **Volume (vwma)** — The 8/17 breakdown occurred on a 2.5× volume spike; VWMA confirms whether the bounce is being absorbed by sellers (price below VWMA = bearish volume flow).

This mix keeps each indicator in a distinct information bucket: trend (3), momentum (3), volatility (1), volume (1) — no redundant oscillators, no duplicate averages.
