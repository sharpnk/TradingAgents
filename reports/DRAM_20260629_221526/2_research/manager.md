### Key Evidence Weighed

- RSI (14) has made a series of lower highs (88 → 71 → 57) while price failed to make a new high. This momentum failure is a classic bearish divergence.  
- Bollinger Bands are extremely wide; price is below the band midpoint ($74.55) and barely above the middle band ($68.15). The center of gravity is shifting lower.  
- The gap to the 10 EMA ($0.57) signals momentum exhaustion, not a supportive pullback. The bounce to $76.89 was immediately sold into—a lower high.  
- Bull’s “golden cross” argument is a lagging indicator; it will persist even if DRAM drops 20% and thus offers no protection.

**2. Sentiment Is Dangerously One‑Sided**  
- StockTwits: **0% bearish** after a 191% rally. Extreme retail euphoria and an absence of skeptics are reliable contrarian signals.  
- The $970M single‑day inflow occurred near the peak, consistent with late‑stage performance chasing (similar to ARKK in 2021).

**3. The Antitrust Lawsuit Is a Material Overhang**  
- The DOJ’s suit targets the exact pricing mechanism behind Micron’s 84.6% margins. Market reaction on June 29 (WDC +6%, DRAM names down) confirms the market is discriminating, not discounting.  
- While past DRAM cartel cases ended in fines, the current DOJ’s appetite for structural remedies is greater; even a 20% margin compression would shatter the bull case.

**4. The $880 Billion Korean Capacity Build Is a Future Oversupply Wave**  
- Samsung and SK Hynix are committing record sums, and the news report explicitly cites “potential oversupply threat.” Even if the capacity is for HBM, history shows memory makers always overbuild during booms, compressing premiums.  
- Hyperscalers will eventually play suppliers against each other to push prices down.

**5. Macro Headwinds Are Building**  
- The yield curve has flattened 45% in 90 days—a pace that has preceded every US recession in the last 40 years. No rate cuts are priced for 2026, raising the cost of capital for debt‑laden fab expansions.  
- DRAM’s P/E of 34.4x is expensive for a cyclical commodity; forward estimates assume flawless execution with no margin of safety.

**6. Risk/Reward Is Decisively Negative**  
Probability‑weighted scenario analysis using the most balanced, evidence‑driven probabilities points to an expected return of **–9.55%** (bull case 20% probability, consolidation 30%, bear 35%, catastrophe 15%). Even under the bull’s optimistic 50% probability, the theoretical upside is a modest 18%, while the downside to the 50‑day SMA (23%) and 200‑day SMA (28%) is substantially larger.
