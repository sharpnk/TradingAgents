**Bull response:** That’s a mathematical artifact of **negative tangible book value**, which is **normal for franchise-heavy companies**.

Wendy’s is primarily a franchisor. They own huge intangible assets — brand value, franchise agreements, goodwill — that don’t appear as equity under GAAP. Over 90% of their stores are franchised. The real measure of leverage is **net debt to EBITDA**: $2.46B net debt / $525M EBITDA = **4.7x**. That’s elevated, yes, but manageable for a cash-flow-generative business.

**Compare to peers:** Yum! Brands (KFC, Taco Bell) runs at 5-6x net debt/EBITDA. McDonald’s is around 3.5x. Wendy’s is in the same ballpark. The company **just refinanced $452M in Q4 2025** — the debt markets are clearly open for them. No covenant breach. No imminent refinancing risk.

**And here’s the bull’s edge:** With **interest rates likely stable** (79% probability of no cuts per prediction markets), the debt burden is fixed. If rates fall even a little, refinancing saves millions. The bear’s “infinite D/E” is a distraction, not a danger.
