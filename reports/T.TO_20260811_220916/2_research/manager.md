### Critical Evaluation of the Debate

The bull analyst constructs a compelling narrative around a beaten‑down free‑cash‑flow story. The key pillars are:

- **FCF generation:** TTM free cash flow of ~$2.4 B, an 11% yield on the current price, driven by the structural completion of the 5G/PureFibre build and a sharp capex decline from $3.65 B to ~$2.5 B annually.  
- **Dividend reset:** The 55% cut removes the unsustainable payout overhang, frees ~$900 M per year for debt reduction, and leaves the new dividend covered ~2× by FCF.  
- **Turnaround catalyst:** New CEO Victor Dodig, a proven capital allocator, with an explicit mandate to deleverage and monetize non‑core assets.  
- **Technical support:** A July‑31 capitulation on massive volume, followed by an eight‑session stabilization at a triple‑confluence support zone ($12.93–12.95) that limits near‑term downside to ~3.5%, while upside to the 50‑day SMA is ~14%.

The bear analyst systematically challenges each element, and **the bear’s arguments ultimately carry more weight for the current decision**:

- **Deteriorating fundamentals:** While FCF is elevated, it is the direct result of capex cuts — not operational strength. Revenue fell 2.2% YoY in Q2, adjusted EPS collapsed 27%, and normalized income dropped from $441 M to $119 M. The “record FCF” is being absorbed by $1.52 B of interest expense and a massive debt pile.  
- **Broken income thesis:** TELUS was a dividend staple. The 55% cut alienated its core shareholder base. The current 10.8% forward yield is not a gift; it reflects the market’s doubt about sustainability. Negative retained earnings (–$2.9 B), negative tangible book value (–$15.5 B), and a 224% debt‑to‑equity ratio leave almost no cushion.  
- **Deleveraging is still a plan, not a reality:** No asset sale has been signed. The bull’s argument that asset monetization and growth optionality can coexist is contradictory — selling crown‑jewel assets would remove the very “free” optionality the bull cites.  
- **Technical posture remains bearish:** Price is below the 10 EMA, 50 SMA, and 200 SMA. MACD is negative and widening. RSI at 34 is weak but not oversold; there is no bullish crossover, no higher low, and every bounce has failed at the 10 EMA. The $12.93 support is the last line of defence — if it breaks, there is no historical floor below it. The purported 3.5% downside is only valid if support holds, and the probability of a break is non‑trivial given the momentum and balance‑sheet risks.  
- **BCE’s beat underscores TELUS’s company‑specific problem:** In a protected oligopoly, TELUS’s underperformance is not a macro headwind; it is an execution and competitive issue that will take quarters to resolve, not a press release.

The bull’s thesis relies on *future* proof — debt paydown, FCF harvest, and re‑rating — while the bear’s thesis is grounded in the *current* reality: a falling knife with no confirmation of a turn. Because the burden of proof lies with the turnaround story and there is zero positive data yet, the balance tilts toward caution.
