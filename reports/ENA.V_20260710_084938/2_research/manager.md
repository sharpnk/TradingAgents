The Bull's argument that hardware turnarounds start exactly this way (ugly balance sheets financing capacity builds that later generate cash) carries real weight.

The $23M current debt due within 12 months towers over the $1.94M cash balance, and the $4-5M quarterly cash burn means the runway is measured in weeks, not quarters. Even the Bull's working-capital conversion math (collecting AR and liquidating inventory) buys one additional quarter at best. The 18.5% effective interest rate on the debt signals that credit markets already view this as a distressed credit — refinancing will be punitive, and an equity raise at distressed prices is the most probable outcome. The Bull's acknowledgment that a $10M raise would dilute by ~20% may understate the problem: if the stock breaks below $5 on the way to the 200-day SMA at $3.62, dilution would be far worse.

The Bear's core argument is the decisive one: the bull case requires revenue growth, margin expansion, AR collection, favorable refinancing, and no distressed dilution to all go right simultaneously. The bear case requires only one thing — the $23M debt wall arriving before cash flow turns positive. And that one thing is a near-certainty.

I therefore recommend Sell. The operational green shoots are real and worth monitoring for re-entry once the balance sheet is restructured. But at $6.63, the risk of total loss or catastrophic dilution is unacceptably high relative to the upside. The market's 53% correction is more likely the beginning of a repricing toward the 200-day SMA than the end of the drawdown.
