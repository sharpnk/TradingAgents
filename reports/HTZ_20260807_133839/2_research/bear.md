
Bear Analyst: # The Bear Case for HTZ: A Squeeze Is Not a Turnaround

I’ll give the bull credit where it’s due: HTZ just reported a decent quarter, and the two-day tape was genuinely violent to the upside. That’s real. But “real” and “durable” are two completely different things. And if we’re going to “follow the data,” let’s follow *all* of it — not just the data points from August 6 and 7.

The bull sees a turnaround. I see a short squeeze inside a solvency story. Those are not the same trade.

---

## 1. The “earnings inflection” is still a loss

Let’s be precise about the Q2 beat:

- **Adjusted EPS: –$0.11 vs. –$0.24 expected** — that’s a smaller loss, not a profit.
- **GAAP net income: +$64M** — on $2.396B in revenue. That’s a 2.7% margin, in a seasonally strong quarter, after a year-ago loss.
- **TTM net income: still roughly –$279M.**

And the quarterly history doesn’t support “turnaround”:

| Quarter | Net Income |
|---|---|
| Q1 2025 | –$443M |
| Q2 2025 | –$294M |
| Q3 2025 | +$184M |
| Q4 2025 | –$194M |
| Q1 2026 | –$333M |

So the *only* profitable quarter in the last five was Q3 — peak vacation season. HTZ is a company that makes money in the summer and gives it back the rest of the year. That’s not an inflection. That’s seasonality.

The TTM loss narrowing from –$747M to –$279M is improvement, yes. But the company is *still losing money*, and it’s doing so with **record rental pricing**. What happens to that “pricing power” when gasoline is above $4 a gallon and the consumer pauses? What happens to the P&L when used-vehicle values roll over again — the exact issue in the securities lawsuit?

---

## 2. “Pricing power” is not a moat — it’s a cyclical snapshot

The bull says record rental pricing despite a smaller fleet proves HTZ has pricing power. I’d flip that: HTZ had record pricing partly *because* it had a smaller fleet. When you can’t put enough cars on the lot, you ration supply and raise prices. That’s not a durable competitive advantage; that’s an inventory shortage.

The car rental business is **capital-intensive, commoditized, and brutally competitive**. Hertz competes with Avis, Enterprise, a wave of used-car sellers, Turo-style peer-to-peer platforms, and — increasingly — ride-hailing and autonomous fleet operators. The “brand moat” doesn’t protect you from a used-car market crash, because the fleet is the product *and* the collateral. If residual values fall, depreciation eats the P&L and the lenders tighten at the same time.

And the robotaxi/Lucid/Uber story? It’s a press release, not a profit stream. The bull admits it’s not in the P&L. Call it optionality all you want — optionality isn’t valuation. HTZ is trading at $2.43 because the balance sheet is distressed, not because the market is ignoring a hidden autonomous-fleet empire.

The bull says 28–30% short interest, 200 million shares traded, RSI above 50, MACD histogram positive. I agree with the mechanics. But let’s think about what a short squeeze actually is:

It’s forced buying from people who *had* to cover. It is not new long-term demand. It is not a fundamental re-rating. It is a temporary imbalance.

The bull wants you to see “room to $3.12.” I see a falling 50-day moving average overhead, plus a stock that just went parabolic after a –40% crash in a single day.
