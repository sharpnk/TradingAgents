## 1. Macroeconomic Backdrop

### Inflation
CPI rose to **333.979** in May (+1.12% over the last 3 months, annualizing to ~4.5%). Core PCE (the Fed's preferred gauge) reached **129.63** in April, gaining a modest +0.24% over the window. The divergence between headline CPI and Core PCE suggests that volatile components (food/energy) are driving headline stickiness, while the underlying trend is more contained. Prediction markets price only a **14% chance** of inflation exceeding 5% in 2026 — down 2.5pp in a week — indicating confidence that the inflation spike is not re-accelerating dangerously.

### Labor Market
Unemployment is rock-steady at **4.3%** (unchanged since March). A stable labor market provides a solid floor under consumer spending and corporate earnings, consistent with the "Goldilocks" narrative.

### Monetary Policy – THE KEY THEME
**This is the single most important macro factor for SPY right now.** New Fed Chair **Kevin Warsh** held his first FOMC meeting on June 17–18, and the market reaction was sharp. The "Chart of the Day" from MoneyShow reported: *"Hawkish talk and hawkish forecasts caused the odds of an interest rate hike to spike!"* The 10-year Treasury yield, which had been drifting higher, spiked to **4.49% on June 17** before settling to **4.46%**. Most tellingly, prediction markets now price an **80% probability of ZERO Fed rate cuts in 2026** — up a massive **+9.2pp in one week** (since the Warsh meeting).

The yield curve (10Y–2Y) has flattened dramatically from **0.49% in March to just 0.27% now** — a 45% compression. A flattening yield curve, especially when driven by the short end rising (reflecting hawkish rate expectations), historically precedes economic slowdowns. This is a cautionary signal for SPY.

### Volatility (VIX)
The VIX currently sits at **16.78**, relatively low but telling a story: it spiked to **22.22 on June 10** (during the initial Warsh/jitter week), then has been settling. The VIX is now below its 30-day average, suggesting some calm has returned, but the June 10 spike shows how sensitive markets are to hawkish Fed communication.
