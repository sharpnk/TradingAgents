**Recommendation**: Underweight

**Rationale**: This debate was genuinely contested, but the bear carried the stronger argument on net. The bull's valuation case is compelling on the surface — a 0.60 PEG and 11.4% FCF yield on a 10%-growing, 89%-gross-margin software franchise is the kind of setup that normally screams "Buy." And there are legitimate green shoots: the MACD crossover, the RSI recovery from oversold territory, David Ricks' insider buying, and Adobe's actual AI product investments (Topaz Labs, Firefly Foundry).

However, the bear successfully exposed structural weaknesses that the bull either minimized or ignored entirely. Three points proved decisive:

1. **The buyback math doesn't work.** Adobe spent $11.28B on buybacks in FY2025 — more than its net income. That means the eye-popping FCF yield and EPS growth are being partially funded by balance sheet erosion, not organic cash generation alone. That is not sustainable, and it undermines the core bull valuation argument.

2. **The balance sheet carries material risk.** Negative $3.5B tangible book value, $14B in goodwill from acquisitions that haven't yet proven their worth, a current ratio of 0.75, and negative working capital. In a SaaS-normal environment this is manageable, but if revenue growth dips below 10% — which the deceleration trend suggests is plausible — liquidity becomes a genuine concern.

3. **The macro and sector headwinds are structural, not cyclical.** The rotation from growth/tech into value and dividend stocks, combined with a higher-for-longer rate environment (78% probability of no further cuts in 2026, sticky core PCE at 2.89%), reprices long-duration equity like ADBE. The stock is 24% below its 200-day SMA, and every rally attempt in the last 12 months has failed at the 50-day. That is not a bottoming pattern — it's a stock under distribution.

The bull's best counter — the insider purchase — was effectively neutralized: David Ricks increased his stake by 130%, but from a small base. A single board member buying a few million in stock does not offset Citi designating ADBE a "least preferred" tech pick or Phillip Securities slashing its target by 47%.

The valuation does provide a floor. This is not a Sell — an 11.4% FCF yield on a genuine cash generator means the downside is not infinite, and if the AI narrative flips positive, ADBE could re-rate quickly. But the weight of evidence tilts bearish enough to warrant reducing exposure. The structural risks are real, and the cheap multiple is partially a mirage sustained by unsustainable buybacks.

**Strategic Actions**: 1. **Reduce position size to an Underweight allocation** relative to benchmark/portfolio weight. Target trimming roughly 25-40% of the current position, selling into any strength that approaches the 50-day SMA (~$234), which has acted as a resistance wall for the past 12 months.

2. **Set a hard stop at $190** — the 52-week low. A break below this level would invalidate the bottoming thesis entirely and confirm the bear's value-trap argument. If breached, exit the remaining position.

3. **Do not add on dips.** The bull's "generational entry point" argument is premature. Wait for concrete evidence of trend reversal before considering rebuilding: specifically, a weekly close above the 50-day SMA on above-average volume, or a fundamental catalyst (e.g., an earnings report showing AI monetization traction or a stabilization in revenue growth above 11%).

4. **Monitor the buyback sustainability.** In the next earnings release, compare operating cash flow to buyback spend. If buybacks again exceed operating cash flow, that is a red flag that strengthens the Underweight thesis and could warrant further trimming.

5. **Key upside risk to watch:** If the MACD histogram continues expanding and the stock clears the 50-day SMA with conviction, the bear thesis weakens and a reassessment toward Hold or even Overweight would be warranted. But do not front-run that signal.
