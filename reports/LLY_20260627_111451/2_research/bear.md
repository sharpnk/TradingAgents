
Bear Analyst: # The Bear Case for LLY: When Euphoria Meets Overextension

Look, I've heard the bull case, and I'll give credit where it's due — the revenue numbers are impressive. Q1 2026 revenue of $19.8B, up 55.6% YoY. Net income of $7.4B, up 168%. Operating margins near 50%. These are legitimate achievements. But here's what the bulls don't want you to see: **you're being sold a story at the exact moment when the technicals are screaming "pause" and the fundamentals are begging for scrutiny.**

Let me walk through why this isn't the time to chase LLY at $1,192.

---

## On Valuation: "Forward PE of 26.9x is reasonable"

**Bull says:** "The forward PE is only 26.9x — a PEG ratio of 1.5, which is cheap for 95% earnings growth."

**Bear says:** That forward PE depends entirely on the assumption that $44.48 EPS is achievable. Let me ask you: what happens when growth decelerates? Because it *will*. The tirzepatide franchise is experiencing a one-time ramp — you're extrapolating 170% EPS growth into perpetuity. Revenue already jumped from $45B to $65B to a TTM of $72B. The law of large numbers is coming. At $1.07 *trillion* market cap, LLY needs to generate $50B+ in net income annually just to justify a 20x multiple. That's double the current TTM net income.

And let's be honest about what "forward PE" really means here. Analysts are notoriously optimistic during boom cycles. When the obesity growth rate inevitably normalizes from 55% to 20-25%, that forward PE of 26.9x suddenly becomes a 40x+ trailing multiple again. The bull case requires perfection — and perfection is priced in.

---

## On Concentration Risk: "It's like Apple and the iPhone"

**Bull says:** "Concentration on tirzepatide is fine — it's like saying everything at Apple hangs on the iPhone."

**Bear says:** That comparison works *only* if you ignore the competitive dynamics. Apple's iPhone moat was built on an integrated ecosystem, switching costs, and brand loyalty that took 15 years to establish. Tirzepatide's moat? It's efficacy data — which can be replicated or surpassed. 

Novo Nordisk's CagriSema is in Phase 3. Viking Therapeutics just initiated Phase 1 for a new obesity candidate. Amgen has its own oral GLP-1 in development. Pfizer is still in the race. The GLP-1 market is going to look like the statin market in 5 years — multiple players, pricing pressure, and margin compression. 

And let's talk about that "pipeline diversification" you're so proud of:
- **Retatrutide**? Phase 3 data not even complete. Polymarket gives it only 12% probability of FDA approval in 2026.
- **Orforglipron (oral pill)**? European launch in H2 2026 *or early 2027*. That's vague, and it targets the out-of-pocket market — a segment that's inherently smaller and more price-sensitive.
- **Centessa acquisition?** Orexin agonists for sleep disorders? That's a $3-5B market, not the $100B obesity market you're leaning on. Nice to have, not a game-changer.
- **Jaypirca EU approval?** Already priced in — that's why the stock jumped 6% on Friday. The easy money is made.

The reality: **85-90% of LLY's growth story is tirzepatide.** When that slows, there's no second engine ready to fire at scale.

## On Debt: "54x interest coverage makes it fortress-grade"

**Bull says:** "Quarterly EBIT of $9.8B covers $180M in interest 54x over. The debt is growth financing."

**Bear says:** Let's talk about *what* that debt is financing. Lilly has $43.37B in total debt — up from ~$16B in 2021. Debt-to-equity of 139%. Yes, interest coverage is excellent *today*. But what happens in a recession? Drug pricing reform? A pipeline failure?

The bull case assumes the growth continues forever. But $10.8B in annual CapEx is a **fixed cost** that doesn't go away if demand softens. Interest rates at 3.63% mean that $43B in debt costs ~$1.5B annually. That's manageable now, but it's 7% of operating cash flow — and that percentage grows if cash flow decelerates.

And let's not forget: **Lilly has negative tangible book value** when you strip out goodwill and intangibles. Book value per share is $34.92, but tangible book is only $19.81. At $1,192 per share, you're paying 60x tangible book for a company that's levered 1.4x. That's not fortress-grade — that's *faith-grade*.
