# NVDA Research Report — Week Ending June 25, 2026

## 3. Semiconductor / AI Sector Analysis

### The Bull Case for NVDA

**a) Micron's Blowout Earnings Validate the AI Cycle**
Micron reported a blockbuster quarter, sending the entire semiconductor complex higher on June 25. Motley Fool's analysis explicitly states *"Micron's Blowout Earnings Could Be Great News for Nvidia Investors"* — a sold-out memory supplier is a powerful confirmation that AI-driven demand for silicon is accelerating, not peaking.

**b) Hyperscaler Capex Continues Unabated**
- **Qualcomm's Investor Day** revealed it has secured **Meta and Microsoft** plus two other hyperscaler customers, targeting **$15B in AI data center revenue**. The very fact that a mobile-chip giant is pivoting aggressively into AI data centers underscores the **massive TAM expansion** happening in the space — and NVDA remains the dominant incumbent.
- **SpaceX** has signed "several large-scale cloud computing capacity deals" over the past month, representing an $82B opportunity. This suggests even non-traditional players are building out AI infrastructure.

**c) Pricing Power Is Evident**
**Apple raising iPad and MacBook prices**, specifically citing "cost of chips amid AI boom" and warning that iPhone hikes could follow, confirms that semiconductor pricing power is broad-based. This bodes well for NVDA's GPU pricing and margins.

**d) Alphabet Joining the Dow**
Alphabet's inclusion in the Dow Jones Industrial Average is a symbolic milestone that reinforces mega-cap tech's dominance. The tech ecosystem that NVDA powers is becoming increasingly embedded in the broader market indices.

### The Bear Case / Risks for NVDA

**a) Competitive Threats Are Materializing**
- **Broadcom built OpenAI's first custom chip** ("Jalapeño") in record time. While the article notes money is "rotating to two other names," this is a clear signal that OpenAI — NVDA's most important customer and partner — is investing in custom silicon alternatives. If successful, it could reduce NVDA's pricing power over time.
- **Qualcomm is pivoting into AI data centers** with a $15B revenue target. While still tiny relative to NVDA (~$130B annual revenue run-rate), the entry of additional competitors could compress margins in the long run.

**b) Power Constraints Threaten Data Center Buildout**
Oilprice.com reports *"The $7 Trillion AI Boom Is Running Out of Power."* A bitcoin-miner-turned-AI-landlord (Bitzero) signed a 110MW lease, but the broader thesis is that **power availability is becoming the gating factor** for AI data center expansion. If hyperscalers cannot get the power they need, GPU orders could face delays.

**c) Hawkish Fed = Growth Stock Headwind**
With an 80% probability of **zero rate cuts in 2026**, the DCF headwind for high-duration assets like NVDA is intensifying. The 10-year yield at 4.41% is not extreme, but if Warsh's first meeting delivers a hawkish surprise (rate *hike* chatter?), NVDA's valuation multiple could compress.

**d) VIX Creeping Higher**
The VIX has risen from ~15-16 in late May to ~18.6 currently. While not alarming, a sustained move above 20 would signal macro stress that typically hits high-beta names like NVDA disproportionately.

## 6. Actionable Insights for Traders

1. **The AI demand thesis is intact and strengthening.** Micron's results are the strongest near-term catalyst. Any NVDA dip related to macro noise should be viewed as a potential buying opportunity if the fundamental AI buildout story remains credible.

2. **Watch the Kevin Warsh Fed narrative closely.** His first FOMC meeting will be pivotal. If the market perceives him as more hawkish than expected, growth stocks including NVDA could face a sharp de-rating. The Polymarket data suggests the market is already pricing this in.

3. **Monitor the Broadcom/OpenAI custom chip situation.** If OpenAI reduces dependency on NVDA's H100/B100 GPUs, it could be a medium-term margin headwind. However, this is likely years away from materially impacting revenue.

4. **The yield curve narrowing (10Y-2Y = 0.31%) is a yellow flag.** Historically, when the curve narrows from a positive level toward inversion, it often precedes economic softening. This is a risk to monitor over the next 1-2 quarters.

5. **Power constraints are an underappreciated risk.** If data center buildout slows due to power availability, it could delay GPU orders. This is a longer-duration risk but worth watching.
